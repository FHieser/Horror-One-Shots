# The Door

<div class="excerpt">
<p>Four months in. Four to go. The nearest human voice is four hours away, and the drills on 4d7 never stop. Tonight, in the middle of the night cycle, a pager goes off. Drone 2 has stopped deep in the tunnels, and the monitors show something that should not be there.</p>
<p class="excerpt-end">A door.</p>
</div>

<dl class="glance">
  <div><dt>Players</dt><dd>4–7 <span>(recommended 4–5)</span></dd></div>
  <div><dt>Duration</dt><dd>1–2 sessions</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Theme</dt><dd>Cosmic horror, paranoia</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Setting</dt><dd>Isolated titanium mine in space</dd></div>
</dl>

## Before Play

Players create their characters on the [Crew](./characters.html) page. Fill every role no player took with an NPC that you play. **Keep the door drawings.** You need them in Act I.

## Act I: The Door

**Opening.** Start with the pager: a sharp, repeating beep in the dark. Then, before anyone wakes, describe the station from the bottom up. Begin in the mines, with dark tunnels, a stalled drone, its light on a door set into raw rock. Move up through the airlock, the empty mechanics bay with its two jeeps, the humming reactor, and the dim corridors. It is late, and almost everyone is asleep. End in the Monitor Station at Drone Control, or with the player Drone Control is calling.

**The Door.** Describe the Door on the monitors. Then hand it over: let the players investigate through the drones and cameras, wake others and argue. The Door does nothing. The captain is called, or comes down on their own.

**Going down.** The captain and a few players drive into the mines to see the Door in person. Let them inspect it. It still does nothing, but **each player sees a different door**. Use the door drawings from character creation: where the handle is, what it's made of, what tech it seems to use. Describe each player's own drawing to them. Let them notice slowly that their descriptions don't match.

If a player interacts with the Door in a way only they perceive (for example, reaching for a handle only they can see), everyone else gets a **headache** and must make a **Sanity Save**.

**The captain takes control.** The captain decides:

- Leave the Door alone and keep it watched.
- Drones and heavy machines must not interact with it, because of possible electromagnetic interference.
- The machines and the daily tasks keep running.

Everyone returns to bed.

**The disappearance.** In the early morning, while everyone sleeps, the captain drives down to the Door alone. Players *can* see it live on the monitors, but the captain must have enough time to reach the Door undisturbed. The captain doesn't answer pager messages. At the Door, the captain draws a gun and shoots out the cameras. After that, there's no more footage.

**Aftermath.** When the players follow, they find the captain's security card and gun lying in front of the Door. There's no sign of the captain. Let everyone who wants to drive down and see it for themselves. The act ends here, at the empty spot in front of the Door.

> **GM commentary**
>
> - This is the most scripted part of the one-shot. Keep it on rails.
> - Throughout the act, the captain calls the shots and steers the players.
> - The door drawings matter most here. Act I is where the differing perceptions should become obvious.
> - By the end, the captain is gone. That leaves a power vacuum the players will want to fill.
> - In later acts, have players or NPCs call back to the captain's decision: *leave it alone, keep it watched, the machines must keep running.*

## Act II: The Vacuum

**Back to work.** After the empty spot, the players will most likely return to their daily tasks. Let them. Play out the routine, the jobs from the [Crew](./characters.html) page, and the conversations at meals. The station keeps running, because the machines must keep running.

**Hallucinations and false pages.** Once routine has settled, the Door starts reaching out. Hand out omens and misleading pager messages as **written notes on paper**, passed privately to individual players. What a player reads, only that player knows. Use the minor omens from the Crew page first, then escalate.

**Mistrust.** Most players are busy with their jobs, and that is where the cracks show: who saw what, who was where, who isn't telling. Then there's the obvious question: who takes the captain's place? Let it be contested. Messages from the company arrive late and say the same thing: *wait, and ignore the Door for now.* Use them to echo the captain's last orders.

**Teams form.** Sooner or later, groups will form around what to do with the Door. Steer towards internal discussions and open conflict between them. From whichever position the players take, steer towards a bigger interaction:

- **Leaving it alone.** Keep following the captain's orders. Watch it, keep the machines running, wait for the company.
- **Blowing it up** causes a massive pressure wave that knocks out most people nearby.
- **Opening it.** At this point players start trying to open the Door *for themselves*.

**Opening the Door in secret.** This should never be discussed openly at the table. Take the player into a separate room and let them try. The pager hints work best here. Don't be too obvious: the player should have an **aha moment** when they work it out. The trick is that the **lights must be out and the cameras must be destroyed or switched off.** When both are true, the player hears a **click** in the Door as it opens.

If the player then opens it, they disappear and are **out of the game**. They must not tell the others what they know. They may still send **pager messages** to the remaining players through the GM, with no spoilers.

**People go missing.** Every disappearance raises the stakes. The Door is clearly dangerous, and someone who went near it is gone. Only the person who vanished knows why, and they can't say. The rest are left with fragments: who went down there, what the cameras showed (or didn't), and strange pages from someone who is no longer there. Use this to feed suspicion and to push the remaining players towards a decision. NPCs can go missing as well.

**Transition.** Anything big involving the Door starts Act III: an explosion, an opening, a disappearance at the Door, or a mass confrontation.

> **GM commentary**
>
> - This is the most open act. Let the players drive, and react.
> - The main tools are private notes and taking players aside to talk to them alone. Both create information gaps between players.
> - Steer towards internal discussion and open conflict.
> - Call back to the captain's decision: *leave it alone, keep it watched, keep the machines running.*

## Act III: Chaos

**The structure falls apart.** At this point there is no script left, and there shouldn't be. The players either turn drastic or go apathetic. Both are valid, so let them happen.

**The teams play out.** Whatever the factions decided in Act II now comes to a head. Plans get carried out, sabotaged or fought over. Player-on-player violence is likely and welcome. Give it room, and let consequences land.

**Drastic visions.** The omens stop being deniable. Use the major omens from the [Crew](./characters.html) page and go further: full hallucinations, shared visions, the dead and missing seen in corridors and on feeds. Pager messages become more frequent and more personal.

**The captain returns.** The captain shows up on tape: in the corridors, in the airlock, near the reactor. Never caught, always just gone. Use the captain to raise the stakes physically, for example:

- the reactor starts to melt down
- life support fails
- the airlock is sabotaged
- the drones and machines turn against the crew

**The end.** The act ends when the crew is broken. Not many players should be left standing.

> **GM commentary**
>
> - Don't try to hold the structure together. React, escalate and keep the pressure on.
> - Apathy is a valid outcome. A player who gives up is still playing.
> - Push for an ending where few survive.

## Epilogue

Two weeks later, a Daedalus security team lands on 4d7. They secure the Door and whoever is still alive. Describe the arrival **for each survivor individually**: where they're found, in what state, and what they're holding onto.

## From the Playtests

Moments from real sessions, to show what the Door can do at the table.

**The blast.** The players blew up the Door. The pressure wave knocked everyone in the tunnels unconscious. When they woke, their air was almost gone and their suits were flashing warnings. They barely made it back up to the station on foot, and their Stress went through the roof.

**Footprints.** On a surface trip to take bio probes, the crew saw a door standing in the open. One player went to it, tried to interact with it, and disappeared. Another player followed their footprints and found that the door had been a hallucination. Behind where it had stood was a huge fault in the ground. Did they fall, or did they actually open the Door?

**The last attempt.** In Act III, as their final option, a player went down to the Door alone and tried to open it. They couldn't. In the epilogue, the security team found their character dead in front of the Door. They had run out of air.
